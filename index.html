Master's Thesis · In Progress

Compute-in-Memory Ising Machine

George Washington University · MS Thesis · 2026–2027

Developing a compute-in-memory Ising machine — a hardware accelerator that solves hard combinatorial optimization problems by relaxing a network of coupled spins toward a minimum-energy state, with the computation carried out inside the memory array itself. The thesis spans both the hardware architecture and a simulation framework for evaluating it.

  • Designing a compute-in-memory architecture that performs Ising-model spin updates within the memory array, avoiding the processor–memory data-movement bottleneck.
  • Building a simulation framework to model the machine's dynamics and evaluate convergence on optimization problems.
  • Situates Ising-machine hardware within quantum-inspired computing — classical accelerators aimed at the optimization problems quantum computers also target.

02 Digital & VLSI

Tiny MIPS CPU — Full ASIC Design Flow

George Washington University · ASIC Design · Spring 2026

A multi-cycle Tiny MIPS processor carried through the complete front-to-back ASIC flow, from RTL to a routed, pad-framed chip. The design executes a ten-instruction subset via a 13-state finite-state-machine controller over a shared 8-bit datapath.

  • Completed the full back end: synthesis, DFT scan-chain insertion, ATPG, place-and-route, and pad-frame integration.
  • Reached 99.49% stuck-at and 98.09% transition-delay fault coverage with Synopsys TetraMAX.
  • Closed timing at 25 MHz with zero violating paths across all 439 timed endpoints.
STACKVerilog/Synopsys DC + TetraMAX/Cadence Innovus/OSU 0.5 µm/0.825 mm²

03 Digital & VLSI

64-QAM Digital Modulator with SPI & CDC FIFO

George Washington University · High-Level VLSI Design · Spring 2026

A 64-QAM digital modulator in Verilog integrating a symbol mapper, an SPI configuration interface, and a synchronous-FIFO clock-domain-crossing bridge with three-domain reset sequencing, verified through SDF-annotated, timing-accurate gate-level simulation.

  • Synthesized on the IBM CMOS8HP standard-cell library, meeting timing at roughly 130 MHz.
  • Diagnosed and resolved gate-level X-propagation by reworking negedge logic to posedge with active-high signals.
  • Debugged SPI task timing, testbench capture windows, and SDF annotation scoping in Cadence Xcelium.
STACKVerilog/IBM CMOS8HP/Cadence Xcelium/SPI/clock-domain crossing
