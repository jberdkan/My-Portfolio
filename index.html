Master's Thesis · In Progress

CMOS Oscillator-Based Ising Machine

George Washington University · MS Thesis · SkyWater 130nm · 2026–2027

Bringing to silicon an oscillator-based Ising machine architecture developed in the lab — a network of coupled CMOS oscillators whose phase dynamics, under sub-harmonic injection locking, settle into a configuration that minimizes the Ising Hamiltonian of an encoded combinatorial-optimization problem. The thesis spans the circuit-level implementation in SkyWater 130nm, a simulation framework for the network dynamics, and a novelty layer extending the original design.

  • Implementing the circuit-level design — oscillator topology, coupling network, and phase-readout — in the SkyWater 130nm open-source PDK.
  • Developing a simulation framework to model the network's phase dynamics and evaluate its convergence on benchmark combinatorial-optimization problems.
  • Designing a novelty layer that extends the original architecture, in collaboration with a senior PhD student in the lab.
  • Work on track to produce a co-authored publication.

02 Digital & VLSI

Tiny MIPS CPU — Full ASIC Design Flow

George Washington University · ASIC Design · Spring 2026

A multi-cycle Tiny MIPS processor carried through the complete front-to-back ASIC flow, from RTL to a routed, pad-framed chip. The design executes a ten-instruction subset via a 13-state finite-state-machine controller over a shared 8-bit datapath.

  • Completed the full back end: synthesis, DFT scan-chain insertion, ATPG, place-and-route, and pad-frame integration.
  • Reached 99.49% stuck-at and 98.09% transition-delay fault coverage with Synopsys TetraMAX.
  • Closed timing at 25 MHz with zero violating paths across all 439 timed endpoints.
